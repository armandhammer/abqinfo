SUMMARY
Nob Hill has two distinct zones: Lower and 
Upper Nob Hill and while their redevelop­
ment potential varies, their goals are aligned. 
Nob Hill wants to preserve its historic 
character and unique attributes, enhance its 
retail viability, and sensitively accommodate 
the anticipated new residential density as a 
result of ART. Nob Hill is distinguished by a 
variety of historic, characteristic and special, 
(even if non-contributing) buildings. With a 
greater concentration of buildings worthy of 
retaining, Lower Nob Hill should focus on 
preservation goals. With more empty parcels 
and properties likely to develop, Upper Nob 
Hill should focus on redevelopment goals.

NETWORKS
Nob Hill is a collection of early 20th cen­
tury developments on either side of Central 
Avenue. A few of these diverge from the 
standard block layout of Albuquerque’s early 
neighborhood grids. Between Girard and 
Carlisle Boulevards, north of Central Ave­
nue, the grid shifts in a few interesting ways. 
This shift creates Monte Vista Boulevard, 
which intersects with Central Avenue and 
Girard Boulevard in a problematic 5-way in­
tersection. Additional, less substantial shifts 
occur further east throughout the neighbor­
hood. These grid shifts limit the directness 
of thru-streets, creating calmer streets for 
low to medium density housing but fewer 
direct vehicular connections. To the west, 
part of the grid is disrupted by the Univer­
sity, however this does not have a significant 
effect in Nob Hill. Because of the grid shifts 
and University, vehicular traffic east-west 
is pushed to major roads, increasing their 
volumes while limiting traffic through the 
neighborhood.
Nob Hill has a relatively complete east-
west bicycle network south of Central, but 
is less complete to the north, due in part 
to disconnection at the University. In the 
north-south direction, the area lacks regular 
bicycle connections. The pattern of existing 
and proposed bicycle facilities creates an 
incomplete network with a series of major 
gaps.

5-WAY TO 4-WAY INTERSECTION
Currently Central Avenue, Girard Boule­
vard, and Mote Vista Boulevard meet at 
a 5-way signalized intersection in Lower 
Nob Hill. The intersection can create hectic 
conditions, resulting in travel delays, and 
presents dangerous conflicts between all 
road users. 
Between 2010 and 2013, 99 collisions oc­
curred at this intersection, including three 
collisions involving bicyclists. This accounts 
for over eight percent of collisions through­
out the Nob Hill study area during this 
period. 
Re imagining this intersection as a 4-way 
intersection by closing off Monte Vista Bou­
levard as designed, would increase safety due 
to a decreased number of potential conflict 
points, facilitate the development of public 
space and pedestrian oriented developments. 
In addition, it would bring congestion 
relief by simplifying the operations at the 
intersection. Whereas a 5-leg intersection 
assigns approximately 20 seconds of time 
for throughput in each direction (over four 
phases), a 4-leg intersection only has two 
phases, each at approximately 43 seconds, 
facilitating the flow of traffic through the 
intersection.
The closing of a 5-way intersection has been 
accomplished successfully in other cities, 
such as Los Angeles and Atlanta, shown to 
the right. In the case of Los Angeles, the 
closure prevents potential conflicts between 
pedestrians and vehicles as vehicles would 
previously enter a residential street at rela­
tively high speeds, while creating a commu­
nity space. In Atlanta, pedestrian activity is 
fostered within the active Midtown District.

 B.03
HEIGHT STANDARDS IN THE IDO 
WILL ENCOURAGE ILL-PROPOR­
TIONED BUILDINGS.
Most zoning codes restrict height in feet, 
such as a maximum of 54 feet. There is 
another system used that measures height 
in the number of stories. Measuring height 
in feet has a number of problems: develop­
ers are encouraged to fit as many stories as 
possible within the height limit, resulting in 
low ceiling heights. It discourages appropri­
ately scaled ground floor retail spaces which 
should be between 16 and 20 feet tall. It 
results in monotonous streets where every 
building is the same height. 
When height is restricted by stories rather 
than feet, it allows developers to determine 
the height of each story according to its 
use and market. Apartment ceiling height, 
for instance, can vary depending upon 
the target market of the unit. Limits can 
carefully control this system so outcomes 
are still predictable and within a reasonable 
range. Historically, many cities developed 
in this manner, resulting in the variety that 
articulation requirements attempt to create.

lots are small, a number of larger lots and lot

be provided within a 2.5 minute walk from

 B.08
CENTRAL AVENUE IN NOB HILL 
LACKS STREET TREES.
Street trees are important in both com­
mercial and residential areas. They provide 
shade and reduce heat islands. They also 
have significant psychological benefits. 
Throughout the Nob Hill main street area, 
very few streets have trees. Many of the 
streets also have limited right-of-way. In 
such cases, street trees can be added within 
the paved area of the street by removing 
sections of concrete, usually within parallel 
parking lanes.
