towards the Rio Grande to conventional 
suburban development west of Coors Bou­

it requires substantial work, West Central 
deserves more focused study. Implementing 
the recommendations from this effort alone 
requires commitment and coordination 
between the City and the community.

VEHICULAR AND BICYCLE NET­
WORKS
Atrisco and West Mesa are very underserved 
areas for walking and bicycling. Lack of con­
nectivity is due to the informal development 
pattern with blocks at a variety of scales 
and sequentially developed streets. While 
most streets have multiple connections, the 
network created is indirect and difficult to 
navigate. The topography also poses chal­
lenges.
As with the Coors District, recommen­

long-term implementation, contingent on

lane prevents additional crosswalks in the 
western end of the area. 
NEIGHBORHOOD STRUCTURE
There is limited neighborhood structure. 
The area around Atrisco has been informally 
developed piece-by-piece in an uncoordinat­
ed manner. The result is an organic pattern 
of streets and uses. There are no clear com­
munity centers. Although there are some 
attached and multi-family housing clusters, 
most of the residential buildings in the area 
consist of detached single-family houses. 
The scale is predominantly single story.
The West Central Avenue neighborhoods 
are in need of physical manifestations of 
their community’s history, cultural identity,

occupies a key geographic location adja­

proximity to the Paseo del Bosque Trail, and 
therefore is the gateway to West Central as 
one crosses the river from the east.

THE ATRISCO STATION AREA COULD SERVE AS 
A GATEWAY TO WEST CENTRAL AND LEVER­
AGE THE PROXIMITY TO THE RIO GRANDE.
A future Atrisco Gateway Park located at the City-owned MR lots at 
Central and the Atrisco Lateral Drainway could create an important 
entrance to the West Mesa community. The new park could serve as 
a marker to explain the area’s settlement history and strong regional 
connections. 
Entering Albuquerque from the west, the idea of a literal gateway 
has been embraced, but the crossing of the Rio Grande and historic 
assets of the area are not clearly identified or expressed. In fact, just 
across the river, the Bio Park Aquarium site has been fenced off from 
Central Avenue, with no pedestrian entrances provided. 
1
On the west side of the river, there is an opportunity to celebrate 
this river relationship directly from historic Route 66 with a number 
of actions:
+
+
Clean up the MR lots on the north side of Central and Atrisco 
and program community events and festivals to begin identify­
ing the setting as a gathering place.
+
+
Design and install gateway elements to mark the area along 
Central Avenue. This does not have to cross the road like the 
gateway east of Coors, but should clearly identify the area.
+
+
Construct a trail head for access to the Rio Grande trails, in 
coordination with the community space.

THE STATION AREA IS RICH 
WITH CULTURE, LOOKING 
FOR PLACES WHERE IT CAN 
BE EXPRESSED.
While there is not a clear physical expression 
of the community's rich heritage, the com­
munities are close-knit and culturally rich. 
The built environment should provide more 
spaces for cultural expression.
A culinary plaza could be established by 
cleaning up the vacant lot on the southeast 
corner of Central at Cypress. The City or 
the local merchants group could sponsor 
community food-related events where local 
eateries and neighborhood food vendors op­
erate out of food trucks or informal stands. 
Additional retail vendors in the community 
could sell from kiosks and local musicians 
be given a place to perform. As the events 
become more popular, the plaza could ac­
quire a permanent community kitchen and 
rest room facilities to provide food service 
incubation.
+
+
The City or community leaders should 
work with the property owner to secure 
use of the Cypress lot for community 
events.
+
+
Pilot a community event featuring food 
and music, possibly associated with a 
holiday.
+
+
Schedule regular events focused on 
local culture, vendors and music, mar­
keted city-wide.

Unlike the state of Central Avenue around 
Coors, at Atrisco the scale of property 
ownership and percentage of locally-owned 
retailers makes incremental, community-
driven improvements more likely. Currently 
most businesses along this portion of the 
corridor are set back from the street sur­
rounded with surface parking lots, many of 
which are underutilized. Without significant 
new demand for retail in the area, rede­
velopment is unlikely to occur, and when 
it does it will be incremental. To assist in 
making the area more vibrant and to help 
spur interest in infill, community-led action 
could highlight local culture around existing 
facilities.
Existing retailers and restaurateurs can be 
encouraged or assisted to re-pave, landscape, 
and utilize their parking areas to further ac­
tivate Central Avenue and their businesses. 
Smaller incubator retail pop-up liners could 
be installed on the edges of underutilized 
parking lots that could screen vehicles and 
help promote the culture of the Atrisco 
Station area. Incubator liners could be built 
inexpensively and provide business startup 
space for the surrounding community.

 B.03
AUTOMOBILE-DEPENDENT DE­
SIGN DISCOURAGES WALKABIL­
ITY AND SMALL BUSINESSES.
Central Avenue west of the Rio Grande is 
designed primarily for automobiles and is 
dangerous for pedestrians and bicyclists. 
While sidewalks exist, they are often located 
next to high-speed travel lanes and inter­
rupted by light poles and other utilities. 
Bicycling along this portion of Central Av­
enue is not safely possible. The automobile-
dominated pattern of development results in 
every business having its own parking lot. 
Main streets in other parts of the City sup­
port multiple small businesses by increasing 
incidental foot-traffic. When people park 
once and walk past multiple businesses, ex­
posure to businesses increases. The develop­
ment pattern and street design are incom­
patible with the community's culture and 
character. Without changes to this situation, 
business viability will remain limited.

 B.04
RETURN-ON-INVESTMENT (ROI) 
IS UNDERESTIMATED IN WEST 
CENTRAL COMMUNITIES.
Because West Central communities have 
generally had lower average incomes than 
other neighborhoods in the City, they have 
been seen as less effective targets for public 
investments. This is often exacerbated 
by lower participation in formal public 
processes. In many places significant public 
returns have been seen from investments in 
under-served communities. However they 
require a different process of engagement 
and evaluation than typically applied in 
other places. 	
g

+
+

of community leaders that meets 
regularly and advises the City on com­
munity investments and the effects of 
investment as seen from their perspec­
tives.
