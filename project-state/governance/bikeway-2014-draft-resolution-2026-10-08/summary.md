### 1 — Chapter comparison

Final adds explicit four-year plan updates and annual map updates, a fuller glossary including buffered/protected lanes and cycle tracks, open-space and user definitions, and safety-in-numbers discussion. This is substantive revision of the introduction, not merely repagination.

### 2 — Chapter comparison

Goals and Policies becomes Goals and Principles. Goal 1 changes from improving cyclist/pedestrian safety to improving/enhancing cycling/pedestrian opportunities. Final adds pilot testing of practices, an explicit 5-percent walking/biking trip target to the use principle, performance measures and more open-space/plan relationships. It establishes NACTO as governing in conflicts with AASHTO in developed urban areas, unless project funding stipulates otherwise. The 5-percent target also occurs in the draft federal-policy discussion; it is not wholly new to the family.

### 3 — Chapter comparison

The final expands open-space trail inventory and ADA context, specifying just over 100 miles managed by Open Space and the limited scope of the 1996 ADA survey. It revises user needs, collision analysis and maintenance reporting/YARDI discussion. It removes the draft assertion that overpasses are crucial at Paseo del Norte/Coors and east I-40/Rio Grande. Draft opportunities-and-constraints PDF45 is a TIFF placeholder, whereas final provides the mapped material.
