      Traffic calming and speed management initiatives.

      Neighborhood Traffic Management Program#

      • Neighborhood Traffic Management Program

        Provides the maintained program overview for addressing speeding and cut-through traffic on residential streets, including eligibility, request procedures, current meetings, completed studies, policy documents, and emergency-route constraints.

      • Submit a Traffic Calming Request

        Explains how residents request a City traffic-calming evaluation, what information the application requires, which streets qualify, and where to submit the online or written request.

      • NTMP Traffic Calming Request Timeline (archived image)

        Illustrates the City’s current Neighborhood Traffic Management Program request process from submission through screening, evaluation, prioritization, and project delivery, clarifying how neighborhood traffic-calming requests advance.

        View the official CABQ ArcGIS item and check for updates.

      • Pending Traffic Requests Map

        Embeds the City’s current traffic-calming request dashboard and links a mobile-friendly map for checking locations and status of pending neighborhood requests across Albuquerque.

        Open the live NTMP dashboard.

      • Traffic-Calming Toolkit

        Describes the City’s full range of traffic-calming tools, with advantages, disadvantages, application guidance, illustrations, and individual references for markings, crossings, chokers, circles, humps, diverters, and other treatments.

      • Completed Neighborhood Traffic Studies

        Indexes more than one hundred current and archived Albuquerque speed, volume, traffic-calming, and neighborhood studies by street, including recent reports and a substantial historical record of local traffic analysis.

      • NTMP Program FAQ and Evaluation Criteria

        Albuquerque’s Neighborhood Traffic Management Program explains how local streets are evaluated for traffic-calming measures, the role of community participation, and the criteria used to prioritize work.

      • NTMP Program Documents and Traffic-Calming Tools

        The City provides the program policy, toolkit, and individual traffic-calming reference documents. These are a better source for process and design choices than isolated project announcements.

      Policy Archive#

      • Neighborhood Traffic Management Program Policy Manual (2015)

        Defines Albuquerque’s Neighborhood Traffic Management Program goals, eligibility, request and evaluation process, prioritization, neighborhood participation, funding, implementation, and traffic-calming strategies for local and collector streets.

        Official City Council project archive · Official City PDF

      • Streets and Traffic Enhancement Program Draft Policy Manual and Resolution (2014)

        Preserves the original STEP proposal and draft resolution that preceded the 2015 NTMP policy, documenting proposed administrative rules, traffic-calming tools, evaluation, neighborhood participation, funding, and legislative lineage.

        Official City Council project archive · Official City PDF

      Citywide Safety Program#

      • Vision Zero Implementation Work

        The Vision Zero implementation page provides the City’s broader safety-program context, including traffic management, automated speed enforcement, and active safety work.

      Bernalillo County Automated Enforcement#

      • Bernalillo County Automated Photo Speed Enforcement

        Lists active and pending camera locations, enforcement rules, citation and appeal procedures, safety rationale, program contacts, and current scam warnings for Bernalillo County’s automated speed-enforcement program.
